The problem

A pipeline nobody actually trusts.

  • The forecast lives in one tab, the activity in another, and the truth in a spreadsheet somebody edited on Friday.
  • A deal "looks" healthy until a rep admits it has been stalled for three weeks.
  • Nobody can answer "what closes this month?" without a meeting.

The approach

One board that tells you the next move.

Cadenza puts every deal on one screen and keeps the context beside it: the stage, the owner, the last touch and the amount. When a deal moves, the totals move with it — so the number at the top is always the number underneath.

The pipeline is the product

One board for the team, one board for the number. Drag a deal to a new stage and the column values and the forecast update in the same motion — no separate report to reconcile.

  • Stage definitions you set once, applied everywhere.
  • Rotting-deal flags when a card sits too long.
  • Weighted and raw totals on every column.

One record, not five tabs

A contact, their company, the deals they touch and every email or call are the same object. Open a name and the history is there — including the notes the last rep left.

  • Company and contact linked automatically.
  • Every activity stamped with owner and date.
  • Fields you add show up on the board and the report.

Quiet rules that keep the board honest

Automations in Cadenza are for housekeeping, not spam. Build them from plain sentences — when this, then that — and watch them run on a log you can read.

  • Triggers on stage, date or field changes.
  • Every run logged with what it touched.
  • Pause any rule without losing its history.

Reports that match the board

Because the report reads the same deals as the pipeline, the closed-won chart can never disagree with the board. Slice by owner, stage or source and the number follows.

  • Win rate, cycle time and average deal size.
  • Filter by owner, source or close date.
  • Export a snapshot, or schedule one weekly.
