The object — 01

Five things to know before you pour.

Material Shape Drip rate Hand-finish Care
  1. 01

    Material

    High-fired stoneware with a dense, vitrified body. It holds its temperature through a slow pour and resists staining, so the clay reads the same after a year of mornings.

  2. 02

    Shape

    A low 60° cone that sits close to the server. The wide mouth keeps the bed even as you pour and the profile stays stable on the rim rather than tipping.

  3. 03

    Drip rate

    One generous central aperture. Flow is set by your pour rather than by a narrow outlet, so you keep control of the pace from first bloom to last drop.

  4. 04

    Hand-finish

    Each piece is dipped, wiped at the rim and fired a second time, so rims stay clean and no two sets match exactly. Small tonal variation is the point, not a flaw.

  5. 05

    Care

    Rinse in warm water, dry it fully, and store the dripper open so air reaches the cone. Hand wash only — a dishwasher is hard on the glaze over time.

Making — 02

Fired, not printed.

Every Kora set is one object made slowly, not a mould pressed a thousand times. Four stages, all by hand, and a week between the first throw and the last check.

  1. 01

    Sourced

    A fine stoneware body, blended for an even wall and a warm base tone, then rested before it is worked.

  2. 02

    Thrown

    The dripper, server and cup are thrown and trimmed by hand, then set aside to dry slowly so the walls stay true.

  3. 03

    Fired

    A bisque firing, then glaze, then a high firing that vitrifies the body and settles the surface into its final tone.

  4. 04

    Finished

    Rims are wiped, bases are sanded flat, and every piece is checked by eye before it is boxed with its filters.

Care — 04

Keep it simple.

Stoneware is forgiving. Treat it like a good pan rather than a delicate thing and it will look better in five years than on day one.

  • Rinse in warm water after each brew, while the bed is still loose.
  • Use mild soap occasionally; skip abrasive pads and scouring powder.
  • Hand wash only — repeated dishwasher cycles dull the glaze.
  • Let every piece dry fully before it goes back on the shelf.
  • Store the dripper open so air can reach the inside of the cone.
  • Avoid sudden temperature changes, like hot water into a cold server.

Questions — 05

Good to know.

Everything a first-time buyer tends to ask.

Which paper filters fit?

Any standard cone-style paper filter sized for one to four cups. The aperture is generous, so a fine or a medium grind both work without the bed stalling.

How much does it brew?

The server holds 600 ml and the cup holds 260 ml, so a single pour makes two generous cups or one tall one. The dripper handles a full 600 ml bed comfortably.

Is it dishwasher safe?

No — hand wash is strongly recommended. Harsh detergents and high drying heat gradually dull the surface, and stoneware prefers a slow, warm wash.

Will my glaze look exactly like the pictures?

Close to it, but not identical. Each piece is dipped and wiped by hand, so tone and pooling vary slightly from set to set. That variation is the signature of the process.

Is this a real shop?

No. Kora is a self-initiated concept project. The brand, glazes, specifications and prices are invented and marked “Concept pricing”; there is no checkout and no product to buy.
